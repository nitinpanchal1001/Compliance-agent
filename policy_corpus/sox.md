## §302 — Corporate responsibility for financial reports
The principal executive and financial officers must certify in each periodic report that they have reviewed the report and that, based on their knowledge, it does not contain any untrue statement of a material fact or omit a material fact. Certifying inaccurate or misleading financial statements is a violation.

## §404 — Management assessment of internal controls
Management must establish and maintain an adequate internal control structure over financial reporting and assess its effectiveness annually. The absence of documented internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR) is a material weakness.

## §409 — Real time issuer disclosures
Issuers must disclose to the public, on a rapid and current basis, material changes in their financial condition or operations. Failing to disclose material events on a timely basis violates this section.

## §802 — Criminal penalties for altering documents
Whoever knowingly alters, destroys, conceals, or falsifies any record or document with intent to impede an investigation shall be subject to penalties. Audit work papers and financial records must be retained for at least seven years; premature destruction is prohibited.

## §806 — Whistleblower protection
A publicly traded company may not discharge, demote, or discriminate against an employee for providing information regarding conduct the employee reasonably believes constitutes a violation. Retaliation against whistleblowers is prohibited.

## §906 — Corporate responsibility for financial reports
Each periodic report containing financial statements must be accompanied by a written certification by the chief executive officer and chief financial officer that the report fully complies with securities laws and fairly presents the financial condition. A knowingly false certification carries criminal liability.

## ICFR — Segregation of duties
Effective internal control over financial reporting requires segregation of duties so that no single individual controls all phases of a financial transaction. Allowing one person to initiate, approve, and record transactions is a control deficiency.
