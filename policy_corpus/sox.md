## §201 — Services outside the scope of practice of auditors
A registered public accounting firm that audits an issuer is prohibited from contemporaneously providing specified non-audit services, such as bookkeeping, internal audit outsourcing, or financial information systems design. Engaging the external auditor for prohibited non-audit work impairs independence and is a violation.

## §203 — Audit partner rotation
The lead audit partner and the reviewing partner must rotate off an issuer's audit at least every five years. Retaining the same lead audit partner beyond the rotation limit is a violation of auditor independence rules.

## §301 — Public company audit committees
The audit committee must be composed of independent directors and be directly responsible for the appointment, compensation, and oversight of the external auditor, and must establish procedures for handling complaints about accounting matters. An audit committee lacking independence or a whistleblower complaint process is non-compliant.

## §302 — Corporate responsibility for financial reports
The principal executive and financial officers must certify in each periodic report that they have reviewed it, that based on their knowledge it contains no untrue statement or omission of a material fact, and that they are responsible for and have evaluated disclosure controls. Certifying inaccurate or misleading financial statements is a violation.

## §303 — Improper influence on conduct of audits
It is unlawful for any officer or director, or person acting under their direction, to fraudulently influence, coerce, manipulate, or mislead the external auditor for the purpose of rendering the financial statements materially misleading. Pressuring the auditor to alter findings is a violation.

## §304 — Forfeiture of certain bonuses and profits
If an issuer must restate financial statements due to material non-compliance resulting from misconduct, the CEO and CFO must reimburse the issuer for bonuses and incentive- or equity-based compensation received during the relevant period. Retaining such compensation after a misconduct-driven restatement is a violation.

## §306 — Insider trades during pension fund blackout periods
Directors and executive officers are prohibited from trading in the issuer's equity securities acquired in connection with their service during a pension plan blackout period. Insider trading during a blackout period is a violation.

## §401 — Disclosures in periodic reports
Financial reports must reflect all material correcting adjustments and disclose material off-balance-sheet transactions, arrangements, and obligations. Concealing material off-balance-sheet liabilities is a violation.

## §402 — Enhanced conflict of interest provisions
It is unlawful for an issuer to extend or arrange personal loans to its directors or executive officers, with limited exceptions. Providing personal loans to executives is a violation.

## §404(a) — Management assessment of internal controls
Management must establish and maintain an adequate internal control structure over financial reporting and assess its effectiveness annually in the annual report. The absence of documented and assessed internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR) is a material weakness.

## §404(b) — Auditor attestation on internal control
The issuer's external auditor must attest to and report on management's assessment of internal control over financial reporting. A missing or adverse auditor attestation on ICFR signals a control failure.

## §406 — Code of ethics for senior financial officers
Issuers must disclose whether they have adopted a code of ethics for senior financial officers and disclose any waivers. Lacking a code of ethics, or failing to disclose waivers, is a violation.

## §407 — Disclosure of audit committee financial expert
Issuers must disclose whether the audit committee includes at least one member who is a financial expert, and if not, why. Failing to make this disclosure is a violation.

## §409 — Real time issuer disclosures
Issuers must disclose to the public, on a rapid and current basis, material changes in their financial condition or operations. Failing to disclose material events on a timely basis violates this section.

## §802 — Criminal penalties for altering documents
Whoever knowingly alters, destroys, mutilates, conceals, or falsifies any record or document with intent to impede an investigation is subject to penalties; audit work papers and records must be retained for at least seven years. Premature destruction of audit records is prohibited.

## §806 — Whistleblower protection
A publicly traded company may not discharge, demote, suspend, threaten, or otherwise discriminate against an employee for lawfully providing information about conduct the employee reasonably believes constitutes a violation. Retaliation against whistleblowers is prohibited.

## §807 — Criminal penalties for securities fraud
Whoever knowingly executes a scheme to defraud any person in connection with the securities of an issuer, or to obtain money or property by false pretenses in connection with such securities, is subject to criminal penalties. Knowingly defrauding shareholders is a violation.

## §906 — Corporate responsibility for financial reports
Each periodic report containing financial statements must be accompanied by a written certification by the CEO and CFO that the report fully complies with securities laws and fairly presents, in all material respects, the financial condition and results of operations. A knowingly false certification carries criminal liability.

## ICFR — Segregation of duties
Effective internal control over financial reporting requires segregation of duties so that no single individual controls all phases of a financial transaction. Allowing one person to initiate, approve, and record transactions is a control deficiency.

## ITGC — IT general controls over financial systems
Financial reporting relies on IT general controls — access management, change management, and operations — over the systems that produce financial data. Uncontrolled access or unmanaged changes to financial systems undermine ICFR and constitute a deficiency.
