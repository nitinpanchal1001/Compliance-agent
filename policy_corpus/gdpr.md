## Art. 5(1)(a) — Lawfulness, fairness and transparency
Personal data shall be processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject. Processing that is hidden, deceptive, or lacks a clear legal basis violates this principle.

## Art. 5(1)(b) — Purpose limitation
Personal data shall be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes. Repurposing data for unrelated uses without a compatible basis is a violation.

## Art. 5(1)(c) — Data minimisation
Personal data shall be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed. Collecting or retaining more personal data than is required for the stated purpose violates this principle.

## Art. 5(1)(d) — Accuracy
Personal data shall be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; inaccurate data must be erased or rectified without delay. Failing to correct known-inaccurate personal data is a violation.

## Art. 5(1)(e) — Storage limitation
Personal data shall be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed. Indefinite or open-ended retention of personal data is not permitted.

## Art. 5(1)(f) — Integrity and confidentiality
Personal data shall be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, using appropriate technical or organisational measures. Storing or transmitting personal data without adequate safeguards violates this principle.

## Art. 5(2) — Accountability
The controller is responsible for, and must be able to demonstrate compliance with, the data protection principles. An inability to evidence compliance (records, policies, controls) is itself a violation.

## Art. 6 — Lawfulness of processing
Processing is lawful only if and to the extent that at least one legal basis applies, such as consent, performance of a contract, a legal obligation, vital interests, a public task, or a legitimate interest. Processing personal data without a valid legal basis is unlawful.

## Art. 7 — Conditions for consent
Where processing is based on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject consented; consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, and as easy to withdraw as to give. Pre-ticked boxes, bundled consent, or an inability to withdraw consent are violations.

## Art. 8 — Conditions applicable to child's consent for information society services
Where information society services are offered directly to a child, processing based on consent is lawful only if the child is at least 16 (or the lower national age, not below 13), or consent is given by the holder of parental responsibility. Offering online services to children without appropriate age and parental-consent checks is a violation.

## Art. 9 — Processing of special categories of personal data
Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade-union membership, genetic or biometric data, health, or sexual orientation is prohibited unless an explicit exception applies, such as explicit consent. Health and biometric records require heightened protection.

## Art. 10 — Processing of data relating to criminal convictions and offences
Personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences may be processed only under the control of official authority or when authorised by law providing appropriate safeguards. Maintaining criminal-record data without legal authorisation is a violation.

## Art. 12 — Transparent information and communication
The controller must provide information to data subjects in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language, and must facilitate the exercise of data subject rights. Obscure, inaccessible, or unanswered rights requests violate this article.

## Art. 13 — Information to be provided where data is collected from the data subject
Where personal data are collected from the data subject, the controller must, at the time of collection, provide identity and contact details, purposes and legal basis, recipients, retention periods, and the data subject's rights. Collecting data without a compliant privacy notice is a violation.

## Art. 14 — Information to be provided where data is not obtained from the data subject
Where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject, the controller must provide equivalent information within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month. Processing third-party-sourced data without informing the data subject is a violation.

## Art. 15 — Right of access by the data subject
The data subject has the right to obtain confirmation as to whether personal data concerning them are processed, access to that data, and information about the processing. Controllers must operate a process to respond to subject access requests, generally within one month.

## Art. 16 — Right to rectification
The data subject has the right to obtain the rectification of inaccurate personal data and to have incomplete data completed without undue delay. Refusing or failing to correct inaccurate data is a violation.

## Art. 17 — Right to erasure ('right to be forgotten')
The data subject has the right to obtain erasure of personal data without undue delay where the data are no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, the data subject objects, or the data were unlawfully processed. A mechanism to honour erasure requests must exist.

## Art. 18 — Right to restriction of processing
The data subject has the right to obtain restriction of processing in defined circumstances, such as while accuracy is contested or processing is unlawful. Continuing to actively process data subject to a valid restriction request is a violation.

## Art. 19 — Notification obligation regarding rectification, erasure or restriction
The controller must communicate any rectification, erasure, or restriction of processing to each recipient to whom the data have been disclosed, unless impossible or disproportionate. Failing to propagate corrections or deletions downstream is a violation.

## Art. 20 — Right to data portability
The data subject has the right to receive personal data they provided in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and to transmit it to another controller where processing is based on consent or contract and carried out by automated means. Locking users into proprietary formats violates portability.

## Art. 21 — Right to object
The data subject has the right to object at any time to processing based on legitimate interests or public task, and absolutely to processing for direct marketing. Continuing direct marketing after an objection is a violation.

## Art. 22 — Automated individual decision-making, including profiling
The data subject has the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal or similarly significant effects, except under specific safeguards. Fully automated significant decisions without human review or safeguards are a violation.

## Art. 24 — Responsibility of the controller
The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures, and review them, to ensure and demonstrate that processing complies with the Regulation. The absence of data protection policies and governance is a violation.

## Art. 25 — Data protection by design and by default
The controller must implement data protection principles, such as data minimisation, by design and by default, ensuring that only data necessary for each specific purpose are processed. Systems that default to collecting or exposing maximal data violate this article.

## Art. 26 — Joint controllers
Where two or more controllers jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they must determine their respective responsibilities in a transparent arrangement. Joint processing without an allocation-of-responsibility arrangement is a violation.

## Art. 28 — Processor
Processing by a processor must be governed by a binding contract setting out the subject-matter, duration, nature, and purpose of processing and obliging the processor to act only on documented instructions and to ensure security. Using a processor without a compliant data processing agreement is a violation.

## Art. 30 — Records of processing activities
Each controller and processor must maintain a record of processing activities under its responsibility, including purposes, categories of data and recipients, transfers, and security measures. Lacking a record of processing activities is a violation for in-scope organisations.

## Art. 32 — Security of processing
The controller and processor must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including pseudonymisation and encryption, confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience. Storing or transmitting personal data without adequate safeguards violates this article.

## Art. 33 — Notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority
In the case of a personal data breach, the controller must notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it. Failure to report a qualifying breach within 72 hours is a violation.

## Art. 34 — Communication of a personal data breach to the data subject
When a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals, the controller must communicate the breach to the affected data subjects without undue delay. Withholding notification of a high-risk breach from affected individuals is a violation.

## Art. 35 — Data protection impact assessment
Where a type of processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals — for example large-scale processing of special categories or systematic monitoring — the controller must carry out a data protection impact assessment beforehand. Proceeding with high-risk processing without a DPIA is a violation.

## Art. 37 — Designation of the data protection officer
The controller and processor must designate a data protection officer where processing is carried out by a public authority, or core activities involve large-scale regular monitoring or large-scale special-category data. Failing to appoint a DPO when required is a violation.

## Art. 38 — Position of the data protection officer
The data protection officer must be involved properly and in a timely manner in all data protection matters, given resources and access, and must not receive instructions regarding the exercise of their tasks or be dismissed for performing them. Side-lining or penalising the DPO is a violation.

## Art. 39 — Tasks of the data protection officer
The data protection officer must inform and advise on obligations, monitor compliance, advise on DPIAs, and cooperate with and act as contact point for the supervisory authority. A DPO without a defined monitoring and advisory mandate indicates non-compliance.

## Art. 44 — General principle for transfers
Any transfer of personal data to a third country or international organisation may take place only if the controller and processor ensure that the level of protection guaranteed by the Regulation is not undermined. Transferring personal data outside the EEA without a valid transfer mechanism is prohibited.

## Art. 45 — Transfers on the basis of an adequacy decision
A transfer may take place where the Commission has decided that the third country ensures an adequate level of protection. Relying on adequacy for a country without a valid adequacy decision is a violation.

## Art. 46 — Transfers subject to appropriate safeguards
In the absence of an adequacy decision, a transfer may take place only where appropriate safeguards are provided, such as Standard Contractual Clauses or binding corporate rules, and enforceable rights and remedies are available. International transfers lacking such safeguards are prohibited.

## Art. 49 — Derogations for specific situations
In the absence of adequacy or appropriate safeguards, a transfer may take place only on a specific derogation, such as explicit consent, contract necessity, or important reasons of public interest. Routine or large-scale transfers cannot rely on the occasional derogations.

## Art. 83 — General conditions for imposing administrative fines
Infringements may attract administrative fines up to EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, with fines required to be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. Severity, intent, mitigation, and prior infringements affect the amount.
