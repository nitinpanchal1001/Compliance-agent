## §164.306 — Security standards: general rules
Covered entities and business associates must ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of all electronic protected health information (ePHI) they create, receive, maintain, or transmit, and protect against reasonably anticipated threats. A security program that ignores known threats to ePHI is non-compliant.

## §164.308(a)(1) — Security management process
Covered entities must implement policies to prevent, detect, contain, and correct security violations, including a risk analysis, risk management, a sanction policy, and information system activity review. The absence of a documented risk analysis is a compliance gap.

## §164.308(a)(2) — Assigned security responsibility
Covered entities must identify a security official who is responsible for developing and implementing the required security policies and procedures. Having no designated security official is a violation.

## §164.308(a)(3) — Workforce security
Covered entities must implement policies to ensure that workforce members have appropriate access to ePHI and to prevent those who should not have access from obtaining it, including authorization, clearance, and termination procedures. Failing to revoke access when employment ends is a violation.

## §164.308(a)(4) — Information access management
Covered entities must implement policies for authorizing access to ePHI consistent with the minimum necessary standard, including access authorization and modification procedures. Granting access without an authorization process violates this standard.

## §164.308(a)(5) — Security awareness and training
Covered entities must implement a security awareness and training program for all workforce members, including security reminders, protection from malicious software, log-in monitoring, and password management. A workforce without security training is a compliance gap.

## §164.308(a)(6) — Security incident procedures
Covered entities must implement policies to address security incidents, including identifying and responding to suspected or known incidents and mitigating their harmful effects. Lacking an incident response procedure is a violation.

## §164.308(a)(7) — Contingency plan
Covered entities must establish data backup, disaster recovery, and emergency mode operation plans so ePHI can be protected and recovered. The absence of tested backups and recovery plans is a violation.

## §164.308(b)(1) — Business associate contracts
A covered entity may permit a business associate to create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI on its behalf only with satisfactory assurances, documented in a contract, that the business associate will safeguard the information. Sharing ePHI with a vendor lacking a business associate agreement is a violation.

## §164.310(a)(1) — Facility access controls
Covered entities must implement policies to limit physical access to electronic information systems and the facilities housing them while ensuring authorized access is allowed. Unrestricted physical access to systems holding ePHI is a violation.

## §164.310(d)(1) — Device and media controls
Covered entities must implement policies governing the receipt and removal of hardware and electronic media containing ePHI, including disposal, media re-use, and accountability. Disposing of drives or devices without sanitizing ePHI is a violation.

## §164.312(a)(1) — Access control
Covered entities must implement technical policies and procedures to allow access to ePHI only to persons or software programs that have been granted access rights, including unique user identification and emergency access. Unrestricted or shared access to ePHI violates this requirement.

## §164.312(a)(2)(iv) — Encryption and decryption
Covered entities should implement a mechanism to encrypt and decrypt ePHI as an addressable safeguard appropriate to the risk. Storing ePHI unencrypted where encryption is reasonable and appropriate is a deficiency.

## §164.312(b) — Audit controls
Covered entities must implement hardware, software, or procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in information systems containing ePHI. The absence of audit logging on systems holding ePHI is a violation.

## §164.312(c)(1) — Integrity
Covered entities must implement policies to protect ePHI from improper alteration or destruction. Lacking controls to detect unauthorized modification of ePHI is a deficiency.

## §164.312(d) — Person or entity authentication
Covered entities must implement procedures to verify that a person or entity seeking access to ePHI is the one claimed. Allowing access without authentication is a violation.

## §164.312(e)(1) — Transmission security
Covered entities must implement technical security measures to guard against unauthorized access to ePHI transmitted over an electronic network, including integrity controls and encryption where appropriate. Transmitting ePHI unencrypted over open networks is a violation.

## §164.316 — Policies, procedures, and documentation requirements
Covered entities must maintain the required security policies and procedures in writing and retain documentation for six years, reviewing and updating it as needed. Operating without documented, retained security policies is a violation.

## §164.502(a) — Uses and disclosures of PHI: general rules
A covered entity may not use or disclose protected health information except as permitted or required by the Privacy Rule. Any use or disclosure outside the permitted bases is a violation.

## §164.502(b) — Minimum necessary standard
A covered entity must make reasonable efforts to limit protected health information to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose of a use, disclosure, or request. Sharing more PHI than necessary violates this standard.

## §164.502(e) — Disclosures to business associates
A covered entity may disclose PHI to a business associate only if it obtains satisfactory assurances that the business associate will appropriately safeguard the information. Disclosing PHI to a vendor without those assurances is a violation.

## §164.508 — Uses and disclosures for which an authorization is required
A covered entity may not use or disclose protected health information without a valid written authorization from the individual, except as otherwise permitted, and most marketing and sale of PHI require authorization. Disclosing PHI for marketing without authorization is prohibited.

## §164.512 — Uses and disclosures for which authorization is not required
A covered entity may use or disclose PHI without authorization for specified public-interest purposes, such as required by law, public health, or law enforcement, subject to stated conditions. Stretching a public-interest exception beyond its conditions is a violation.

## §164.514(a) — De-identification of protected health information
Health information is not individually identifiable only if it has been de-identified by removing specified identifiers or via expert determination. Data containing names, Social Security numbers, or medical record numbers remains PHI and is subject to full protection.

## §164.520 — Notice of privacy practices
An individual has a right to adequate notice of the uses and disclosures of PHI that may be made by the covered entity and of the individual's rights and the entity's legal duties. Failing to provide a notice of privacy practices is a violation.

## §164.522 — Right to request privacy protection for PHI
An individual has the right to request restrictions on uses and disclosures and to request confidential communications, and covered entities must honor certain restrictions, such as for services paid out of pocket in full. Ignoring a required restriction request is a violation.

## §164.524 — Access of individuals to protected health information
An individual has the right to inspect and obtain a copy of their PHI in a designated record set, generally within 30 days and in the form and format requested if readily producible. Denying or unreasonably delaying a valid access request is a violation.

## §164.526 — Amendment of protected health information
An individual has the right to request amendment of PHI in a designated record set, and the covered entity must act on the request within 60 days. Refusing a valid amendment request without a permitted ground is a violation.

## §164.528 — Accounting of disclosures of protected health information
An individual has the right to receive an accounting of certain disclosures of PHI made in the six years prior to the request. Being unable to account for disclosures is a violation.

## §164.530(b) — Training
A covered entity must train all workforce members on its privacy policies and procedures as necessary and appropriate for them to carry out their functions. An untrained workforce is a compliance gap.

## §164.530(j) — Documentation retention
A covered entity must retain required privacy documentation for six years from the date of its creation or the date it was last in effect, whichever is later. Failure to retain compliance documentation for six years is a violation.

## §164.404 — Notification to individuals of a breach
Following discovery of a breach of unsecured PHI, a covered entity must notify each affected individual without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. Delayed or absent individual breach notification violates the Breach Notification Rule.

## §164.406 — Notification to the media
For a breach of unsecured PHI affecting more than 500 residents of a state or jurisdiction, the covered entity must notify prominent media outlets without unreasonable delay and within 60 days. Omitting required media notification for a large breach is a violation.

## §164.408 — Notification to the Secretary
A covered entity must notify the Secretary of HHS of breaches of unsecured PHI — contemporaneously for breaches of 500 or more individuals, and annually for smaller breaches. Failing to report breaches to HHS as required is a violation.

## §164.410 — Notification by a business associate
A business associate must notify the covered entity of a breach of unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery, identifying the affected individuals. A business associate that conceals or delays breach notice is in violation.
