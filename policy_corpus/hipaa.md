## §164.502(b) — Minimum necessary standard
A covered entity must make reasonable efforts to limit protected health information (PHI) to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose of a use, disclosure, or request. Sharing more PHI than necessary violates this standard.

## §164.508 — Uses and disclosures for which an authorization is required
A covered entity may not use or disclose protected health information without a valid written authorization from the individual, except as otherwise permitted. Disclosing PHI to third parties for marketing without authorization is prohibited.

## §164.514(a) — De-identification of protected health information
Health information is not individually identifiable only if it has been de-identified by removing specified identifiers or via expert determination. Data containing names, Social Security Numbers, or medical record numbers remains PHI and is subject to full protection.

## §164.308 — Administrative safeguards
A covered entity must implement administrative safeguards, including a security management process, workforce security measures, and risk analysis, to protect electronic PHI. Lack of a documented risk analysis is a compliance gap.

## §164.312(a)(1) — Access control
Covered entities must implement technical policies and procedures to allow access to electronic PHI only to persons or software programs that have been granted access rights. Unrestricted or shared access to PHI violates this requirement.

## §164.312(e)(1) — Transmission security
A covered entity must implement technical security measures to guard against unauthorized access to electronic PHI being transmitted over a network, including encryption where appropriate. Transmitting PHI unencrypted over open networks is a violation.

## §164.530(j) — Documentation retention
A covered entity must retain required documentation for six years from the date of its creation or the date it was last in effect, whichever is later. Failure to retain compliance documentation for six years is a violation.

## §164.404 — Notification to individuals of a breach
Following the discovery of a breach of unsecured PHI, a covered entity shall notify each affected individual without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. Delayed or absent breach notification violates the Breach Notification Rule.
